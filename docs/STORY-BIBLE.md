# STORY BIBLE — *The Lark and the Quiet Hand*
**Working title of our Dragon Quest V.** Design document only. No code.
Read `docs/DQV-RUBRIC.md` first; this file exists to serve rubric item **7. HEART** and the Story & Voice checklist.

Target length of play: **3 hours 30 minutes to 5 hours** for a competent ten-year-old, including getting lost,
searching pots, and one wipe. Act I ~80 min, Act II ~110 min, Act III ~90 min. Every dungeon is 12–22 minutes.
No grinding is ever required; the wagon and monster friends cover any shortfall.

**Voice law.** Warm, witty, gently British. Every monster's name is a pun. Every NPC is a person in two lines.
Nobody is cruel for fun. Adults are allowed to be silly; children are allowed to be brave. The hero never speaks —
he *does* things, and the people around him say what he cannot. When in doubt, cut the sentence in half and put a
joke where the second half was.

---

## 1. The premise, as a child would retell it

*You're a little boy travelling the world in a wagon with your dad, who is the strongest man alive. One night
you and two girls sneak into a haunted house, find a kitten, and one of the girls ties her ribbon round your
wrist so the dark knows you belong to somebody. Then a very polite bishop catches your dad, because his own
little boy died and he has decided nobody is ever going to be lost again — he turns people to stone instead, and
calls it keeping them safe. Your dad won't stay kept, and he's gone. You grow up in the bishop's quarry. You get
out, your kitten (now a lion) knows you by the ribbon, and you come home and find out your dad was a king. You
choose which of those two girls to marry, and then the bishop turns YOU to stone. Nine years later your own kids
chip you out, and it turns out one of them is the
real hero of the whole story, and your mum has been asleep in a glass lantern the entire time, waiting for you.*

## 2. The cast

Ages given at first appearance / at Act II / at Act III where it matters.

### The hero — **BRAM** (default name; fully player-nameable, 8 characters)
- **Age:** 6 in Act I, 16 in Act II, 26 in Act III.
- **Look:** A round-headed boy in a too-big green travelling cloak with a green ribbon knotted round his left
  wrist, who grows into a broad, sun-browned young man still wearing the same frayed ribbon.
- **Speech tic:** He is silent. His tic is *physical* — when he agrees he nods twice, fast; when he is frightened
  he takes hold of his own left wrist. Every cutscene must use this. NPCs read him aloud: "He's doing the wrist
  thing. He's scared. Right — I'll go first."
- **Want:** To be allowed to help. He is the son of the strongest man in the world and he is very, very small.
- **Most memorable line:** He has none, and that is the point. His one "line" is the ribbon: in the final
  cutscene he unties it and knots it around his daughter's wrist. The screen holds. The player supplies the words.

### The father — **SIR HALVARD BELLWETHER**
- **Age:** 41.
- **Look:** An enormous shaggy bear of a man in a patched blue surcoat, with grey coming in at the temples and a
  greatsword he carries like a walking stick.
- **Speech tic:** Calls his son **"lad"**, never by name, and ends instructions with a small courteous
  **"if you would."** Understates catastrophe: a cave-in is "a spot of bother."
- **Want:** To find his wife Elowen without letting his son find out that he is looking. Six years of "detours."
- **Secret:** He is the King of Ambergarde, travelling as a plain knight. Bram learns this from Papa's Letter (B12).
- **Most memorable line:** *"Look at the sky, lad. Not at me. It's a very good sky tonight, if you would."*

### Childhood friend & marriage candidate #1 — **WILLOW PYE**
- **Age:** 8 / 18 / 28.
- **Look:** Straw-blonde plait, sunburnt, one green ribbon in her hair and one missing, permanently in a fishing
  smock with a slingshot in the pocket and a scab on at least one knee. Grown: an apron, and a whip on the hook.
- **Speech tic:** Answers questions with instructions. "Are you scared?" — "Hold my hand and mind the third
  step, it's rotten." Says **"right then"** before doing anything brave or stupid, and "Come ON" when you dawdle.
- **Want:** To go somewhere. Anywhere. She has run the Contented Herring since she was fourteen and she has
  never once been further than the headland.
- **Most memorable line:** *"I gave you half a ribbon when you were six. I've been holding the other half in my
  apron pocket for ten years like an absolute idiot. Right then. Let's go and get that pearl for her."*

### Childhood friend & marriage candidate #2 — **SERA FAIRWEATHER**
- **Age:** 7 / 17 / 27.
- **Look:** Dark-eyed and small, dressed in the sort of good clothes you cannot climb in, and always slightly
  ahead of her chaperone.
- **Speech tic:** Formal, apologetic, and quietly devastating — she says **"I'm sorry, that was rude of me"**
  immediately *after* saying something perfectly true that nobody else dared say.
- **Want:** To be asked what she wants. Nobody has, in seventeen years, including her adoring father.
- **Most memorable line:** *"Everyone is so careful with me. You dragged me up a haunted staircase when I was
  seven and you didn't ask if I minded. It's the kindest thing anyone has ever done for me."*

### The loyal old retainer — **BARTY MARROW (Bartleby)**
- **Age:** 58 / 68 / 78. Once a palace guard of Ambergarde; small, and made of granite.
- **Look:** A small, wide, bald old man in an apron worn over armour, carrying a ladle in his belt where a
  dagger should be.
- **Speech tic:** Cooking metaphors for absolutely everything, and calls the hero **"little master"** even when the
  little master is a twenty-six-year-old king. "You don't salt a stew before you've tasted it, little master."
- **Want:** To hand the house keys back to the right person before he dies. He has dusted an empty cottage for
  ten years.
- **Most memorable line:** *"I kept the fire in. Every night for ten years, little master. Wasn't sure who for.
  Turns out it was for you."*

### The rival — **PRINCE ADALBERT OF CODDLESTON ("Bertie")**
- **Age:** 8 / 18 / 28.
- **Look:** A pear-shaped boy in ermine two sizes too grand, who grows into a pear-shaped man in armour that
  finally fits.
- **Speech tic:** The royal **"we"**, plus a title he recites in full — and the title gets *shorter* every time
  he says it across the quarry chapters, which is the whole joke and the whole arc. Ends as just "Bertie."
- **Want:** To be worth the fuss people make of him. Discovers, in a stone quarry, that he isn't yet.
- **Most memorable line:** *"We are Adalbert Ludovic Fitzhugh, Ninth of Coddleston, Keeper of the Shallow
  Ford— … Bertie. It's Bertie. Pass me the other end of the rock, would you."*

### The villain — **BISHOP MORTMAIN** of the Order of the Quiet Hand
- **Age:** 60s, looks older.
- **Look:** A thin, immaculately kind-faced old priest in dove-grey, with soft hands and a small glass lantern
  hanging from his belt, and he never once raises his voice.
- **Speech tic:** Speaks to everyone as though they were a patient he is about to make comfortable — **"there
  now"**, **"gently"**, **"that's better"**. Never says "kill" or "dead"; says **"lost"**, **"kept"**, **"quiet."**
- **Want:** For nothing to ever be lost again. His son Tobin died of the coughing sickness at seven; he prayed;
  heaven said nothing; so he found a thing sleeping under the world, **MALGRIM**, that eats sorrow and grants
  wishes, and he has been feeding it for forty years. Stone does not cough. Stone does not go away.
- **Most memorable line:** *"I have never killed anybody, child. Not one soul. I have only ever kept them. Come
  and see how gently I keep them, and then tell me I am the wicked one."*
- **His one crack:** Halvard (B9) would not be kept, and the stone let him go. Mortmain has told himself for ten
  years that this was Halvard's choice. At B25 Rowan does not let him keep telling himself that.

### The mother-mystery — **QUEEN ELOWEN**, last of the Skyborne
- **Age:** 25 when taken, while Bram was a baby. Still 25 when found — she has not aged a day inside the lantern.
- **Look:** A slight woman with feather-pale hair, in a robe that moves as though there were wind indoors, who
  glows faintly, like a window with a candle behind it.
- **Speech tic:** Speaks in present tense about things long over — "Bram is asleep upstairs. Don't wake him."
  Her sense of time is broken and it is heartbreaking rather than spooky.
- **Want:** To finish the lullaby she was singing when they took her. She is still on the third line.
- **Most memorable line:** *"…Oh. Oh, you got so tall. I only put you down for a minute."*

### The comic voice — **BOBBLE the Gloop** ("It's BOBBLE.")
- **Age:** Claims 400. Is possibly 4.
- **Look:** A wobbling blue teardrop Gloop the size of a footstool, with two enormous eyes and a monocle he
  does not need and cannot keep on. (Gloop is the game's mascot species — MONSTER-BIBLE #1.)
- **Speech tic:** Short words, always in the third person, with **one** enormous word per scene used *almost*
  correctly. Begins roughly one sentence in four with **"Frankly—"**. Everyone in the world gets his name wrong
  and he corrects them every single time, forever.
- **Want:** To be described, one day, by somebody, as "the brave one."
- **Most memorable line:** *"Frankly, Bobble has never been so terrified in his life. Bobble is going
  in first. Bobble would like it noted that these two facts are related."*

### Supporting cast (small but load-bearing)
- **PIP** — the Sunspot Cub found at Cobwell Manor (player-nameable): our Dragon Quest V sabre-cat. A ginger
  kitten with two tiny sabre teeth in Act I; runs off into the dark when Halvard falls; in Act II a huge wild
  golden **Sunmane** who stops mid-roar when he sees Willow's ribbon on Bram's wrist (B11b); a lion the size of a
  pony in Act III, who still tries to sit on the hero's lap. He mews in subtitles (VOICE-BIBLE).
- **ROWAN & LINNET** — the hero's twins, 9 in Act III, both nameable. Rowan is the careful one who asks
  questions; Linnet is the one who has already climbed the thing. **One of them draws the Larksteel Sword** —
  by default Rowan (the son), matching the Skyborne prophecy of a grandchild of the Lark; Linnet gets the
  Larkweave Cloak and the biggest magic in the game, and is never once treated as second.
- **LORD RUDOLPHO FAIRWEATHER** — Sera's father. A vast, weeping, generous merchant prince who hugs strangers.
- **GRANDMOTHER NETTLE** — the ghost lady of Cobwell Manor. Not evil. Just very cross about the dust.
- **PRU FAIRWEATHER** — Sera's blunt younger sister (14 in Act II). Not a bride choice. Says "Noted."
- **PAPA'S WAGON & PARSNIP** — Halvard's wagon and its cart-horse: lost at B9, mended at B12, home to every monster friend.
- **DIGBY** — a Barrowmole who digs the way out of the quarry with you (B10).
- **BARON POMFREY STODGE** — the comic rival suitor. A plumed hat taller than he is and a poem he wrote himself.

## 3. Beat sheet — 27 beats

Flags use the `chN.thing` convention and are set via `__DQ.flag()`. Every beat names its map.

### ACT I — THE BOY AND HIS FATHER (~80 min)

**B1 · The cottage at Puddlewick** — *map: `hollybank`*
DOES: name the hero; get out of bed; search four containers (the tutorial that teaches *everything is
searchable*); find Papa's boots under the bed and bring them to him.
FEELS: safety. You are small and the ceiling is low and it is warm.
FLAG: `ch1.awake`

**B2 · Puddlewick village** — *map: `puddlewick`*
DOES: talk to nine villagers, none of whom will sell you anything because you have no money (running joke);
at the lane Papa hands you 30 G ("Don't spend it all on one sword, lad"); climb into **Papa's wagon**, pulled by
**Parsnip** the cart-horse; watch Papa flatten three monsters on the road out in one swing each.
FEELS: pride. Your father cannot be beaten, and everyone here knows it.
FLAG: `ch1.left_home`

**B3 · The Long Lane** — *map: `aldenmoor` (the Long Lane)*
DOES: your first battles (Papa is in the party and will not let you die); first chest; a Gloop ambushes
the wagon, surrenders instantly, and asks to come along. **Bobble joins.**
FEELS: delight. You have a friend and he is ridiculous.
FLAG: `party.bobble`

**B4 · Saltmarrow & the Contented Herring** — *map: `saltmarrow`, `contented_herring`*
DOES: meet **Willow** breaking up a fight she started; meet **Sera** in the inn parlour with a chaperone,
being bored to death politely; sleep (first inn ceremony).
FEELS: the world got bigger by two people.
FLAGS: `ch1.met_willow`, `ch1.met_sera`

**B5 · Saltmarrow at dusk** — *map: `saltmarrow` (dusk)*
DOES: hear five separate villagers give five contradictory accounts of Cobwell Manor, up in the Whispering Wood;
find the poster for a lost ginger kitten; take Willow's dare while Sera, to everyone's astonishment, invites herself along.
FEELS: mischief. The first time you deliberately do not tell your father something.
FLAG: `ch1.dare_taken`

**B6 · Cobwell Manor** — *map: `cobwell_manor` (dungeon, ~18 min)*
DOES: children's party (Bram, Willow, Sera as guests at child stats, and Bobble — no adults, low-stakes combat);
carry the candle, find three music boxes, step the ballroom dance (room-by-room plan: WORLD-BIBLE §4); find
**Pip** the Sunspot Cub in a hatbox in the nursery. **On the Long Stair, when the draught blows the candle out,
Willow tears her ribbon in half and ties one half round the hero's wrist.** Boss: **Mumbleroot the Grudge** in
the belfry. After, **Grandmother Nettle**, lady of the house, delivers her rant about the dust, and gives you
the treasure for tidying up.
FEELS: fear beaten *together*. This is the memory the whole game is built on.
FLAGS: `ch1.manor_cleared`, `ch1.ribbon`, `party.pip`

**B7 · Saltmarrow harbour, morning** — *map: `saltmarrow`*
DOES: say goodbye — walk to the wagon past both girls; Sera's carriage leaves north, Willow stays on the
quay pretending to count crates.
FEELS: the first loss, and it is a small one, which is why it hurts for the next twenty years.
FLAG: `ch1.farewell`

**B8 · Coddleston Castle & the Coddleston Moor** — *map: `coddleston`, `aldenmoor` (Coddleston Moor)*
DOES: Papa shifts the rockfall on the Coppermill Pass like furniture; meet **Prince Bertie** at his own insufferable birthday; Papa is hired as his bodyguard; on the moor,
grey-masked **Quietlings** take Bertie off the road and Papa gives chase with you on his back.
FEELS: dread arriving inside something warm and gold-lit.
FLAGS: `ch1.pass_open`, `ch1.bertie_taken`

**B9 · The Grey Ruins** — *map: `grey_ruins` (broken abbey on Coddleston Moor)*
DOES: a scripted encounter the game will not let you lose *or* win: three turns at most, and the window says
plainly *"You can't reach him."* so no child thinks they failed. Mortmain offers Halvard the deal; Halvard
kneels; Mortmain turns him to stone — and because Halvard will not stop fighting inside it, the stone cannot
hold him, and he is gone. (Mortmain is shaken: in forty years nobody has ever refused to be kept.) Pip bolts
into the dark. **THROAT-TIGHTENER #1.** Cut to the quarry gate closing.
FEELS: the floor of the world goes.
FLAGS: `ch1.halvard_fallen`, `ch2.start`

FEELS: pity for the monster, which is the hardest feeling in the game and the reason it is a good game.
FLAGS: `ch3.mortmain_fallen`, `ch3.malgrim_fallen`

DOES: Elowen spends the last of her Skyborne light; every statue in the garden softens at once, hundreds of
people waking up mid-sentence; **your wife wakes last**, nine years behind everybody else, and asks whose
children those are.
FEELS: the family, finally, all in one frame.
FLAG: `ch3.spouse_freed`

**B27 · Ambergarde — coronation and epilogue** — *map: `ambergarde_keep`, `ambergarde`*
DOES: free-walk the castle and town talking to *every* returning NPC (see §7) — and, on the Sunlark, anywhere else;
then the ending scroll.
FEELS: the specific happiness of a story that ended where it started, with more people in it.
FLAG: `game.cleared`

## 4. The three moments engineered to tighten a grown-up's throat

### T1 — *"Look at the sky, lad."* (B9, the Grey Ruins)
**Setup.** Halvard has beaten forty Quietlings. He is on one knee, breathing like a bellows, and winning.
Mortmain does not fight him. He simply walks out holding a child in each hand — Bertie, and Bram — and says,
kindly, "There now. Put it down."
**Camera.** Locked wide and low, from the ground beside Bram, so Halvard is enormous and framed against the
sky. When Halvard drops the sword the camera does **not** cut — it slowly, slowly pushes in on Bram's face for
nine full seconds while, out of focus behind him, blurred shapes move. The player is not shown the death. The
player is shown the boy watching it, out of focus, in the corner of the frame.
**The line.** Halvard, before he kneels: *"Look at the sky, lad. Not at me. It's a very good sky tonight, if
you would."* Bram's model turns his head up. **He grips his own left wrist.** The ribbon is in shot.
**Music.** `overworld` is playing as they arrive — brass, pomp, safety. On Mortmain's first word the brass
**stops dead, mid-bar, no resolution.** Silence except wind and one child crying somewhere. Eleven seconds of
no music at all. Then a single unaccompanied oboe plays the first four notes of the family theme (`family.broken`) —
and gets it wrong, dropping a semitone, and stops. That's the whole cue. Do not add strings. Do not swell.
**After.** Hard cut to black. Sound of a very large gate closing. Chapter card: *"Ten years pass."*

### T2 — *"Hello, Papa."* (B20, the Stone Garden)
**Setup.** The player has spent six minutes as a nine-year-old girl with no weapon, hiding behind plinths.
She reaches two statues holding hands. She has a rock.
**Camera.** Fixed, eye-level **for a nine-year-old** — i.e. low, looking up at the statues, for the entire
sequence. The player mashes confirm; each press is a chip of stone and a small screen-shake; there are eleven
presses and the eleventh is slower than the others on purpose. On the crack, the camera finally rises to
adult eye-level for the first time — the whole scene *grows up* with him.
**The line.** Stone flakes off a hand. The hand moves. Linnet, not looking up, still hitting it:
*"Rowan. Rowan, get the other one. …Hello, Papa. I'm Linnet. You've been asleep."*
**Music.** Total silence under the chipping — just stone, stone, stone, and a child breathing. On the crack:
the family theme (`family.whole`) arrives in **full strings for the first time in the game**, and it finally plays the
resolving note the oboe couldn't reach in T1. Same four notes. Correct, this time. If a player has forgotten
T1 consciously, their body will not have.
**After.** No level-up fanfare, no chest jingle. The party menu quietly gains two rows.

### T3 — *"You got so tall."* (B24, the Whistfell Undercroft)
**Setup.** A hundred dark lanterns. One lit. Inside it, small and asleep and twenty-five years old, Elowen.
**Camera.** Handheld-slow orbit around the lantern, warm light on every face in the party in turn — the
twins, Barty, Bobble, Pip with his ears flat, the wife's empty place in the formation. Then a **locked two-shot**: mother sitting,
son kneeling, nothing else in frame, held for a long time with no cuts.
**The line.** She wakes mid-lullaby, on the third line, and finishes it. She looks at the huge bearded man
kneeling in front of her and says, brightly, *"Hello. Have you seen my baby? He's just there — he's asleep
upstairs, don't wake him."* The hero cannot speak. He does the only thing he can: **he holds up his left
wrist with the ribbon on it.** She looks at it. Then: *"…Oh. Oh, you got so tall. I only put you down for a
minute."*
**Music.** Her lullaby, sung wordlessly by a single synthesised voice over harp, is the melody the player has
already heard **three times without knowing what it was**: as the C section of Puddlewick's `village` theme in B1, as what
the music box plays in Cobwell Manor, and hummed by an NPC in Saltmarrow. Here it is finally heard whole, with
its words. That's the trick. Plant it in Act I; pay it here.
**After.** Barty is crying loudly and unattractively and Bobble is offering him a handkerchief that is part
of Bobble.

## 5. The ten biggest laughs and exactly where they land

1. **"It's BOBBLE."** (B3, and then forever.) Every single character gets his name wrong — Bubble, Wobble,
   Bobbin, Barbara — and he corrects them, every time, for the entire game. The final
   pay-off: at the coronation the herald announces him **perfectly**, and Bobble has to sit down.
2. **The Puddlewick economy.** (B2.) Nine villagers, all with things to sell, none of which you can afford
   because you have nought gold. The blacksmith shows you a sword, looks at your face, and puts it away
   again, slowly, without a word.
3. **The lying signposts.** (B3 onward.) Every third signpost in the world is wrong, and the fourth one says
   "IGNORE THE OTHER SIGNS — Puddlewick Parish Council." A child will read every sign in the game hunting
   for these.
4. **Grandmother Nettle's grievance.** (B6.) The terrifying lady ghost of Cobwell Manor turns out to be
   furious about *dust*, delivers a four-line rant about the standards of modern haunting, and gives you a
   treasure for tidying up. "In MY day a spectre had *dado rails*."
5. **The Herring's parrot.** (B4, B13.) A parrot that only ever repeats the innkeeper's side of arguments with
   his wife. Ten years later, in B13, the innkeeper is upstairs ill and the parrot is still going, and Willow
   says, without turning round, "Yes, Dad."
6. **Bertie's shrinking titles.** (B10.) The running gag with an arc: five recitations, each shorter, ending in
   "Bertie. It's Bertie." A child will notice the pattern by the third one and start saying it along.
7. **Lord Rudolpho's four conditions.** (B14.) (i) Bring the Tide Pearl. (ii) Be brave. (iii) Be kind.
   (iv) Be able to eat *at least* four helpings, because in this house that is not optional. He then hugs you
   and weeps for forty seconds while you cannot move.
8. **The suit fitting.** (B17.) Barty, a tailor and Bobble all give contradictory instructions while the
   hero stands on a stool; the player is given four wedding-outfit options and one of them is objectively a
   crime. Whichever you pick, Bertie says "Bold." (Your choice is remembered and appears in the epilogue
   portrait forever.)
9. **Bobble negotiating a recruitment.** (Any B22 recruitment, ~1 in 6 chance.) He tries to persuade a
   monster to join, is comprehensively insulted about his shape, and reports back: "Frankly, the interview
   went extremely well and I have decided we don't want him."
10. **The twins are not scared of anything.** (B21–22.) Their first battle: a monster does its big scary
    entrance and Linnet says "Oh, he's *lovely*," and tries to keep it. Every boss thereafter gets one line
    from a nine-year-old that completely deflates it. Rowan, to the final boss's monologue: "That's very sad.
    Are you nearly done, though?"

*(Bonus running gag for the pot-searchers: roughly one in four searchable containers holds an item; the rest
hold a one-line joke, and eleven of them across the world hold **a sock**. Finding all eleven socks gets you the
Sock of Considerable Power, which is genuinely the second-best accessory in the game.)*

## 6. How the bride choice actually works

**Both are childhood friends.** This is the single design change that makes the choice hurt more than the
original ever could. Both girls were in Cobwell Manor with you when you were six. Both remember the ribbon. Neither is
a stranger.

**The mechanical frame (B15–B16):**
1. Lord Rudolpho's condition is public: bring the Tide Pearl and you may ask for Sera's hand.
2. **Willow joins your party for the Sighing Grotto** and fights beside you for twenty minutes to help you earn
   it. She is a *good unit* — the player will grow attached mechanically as well as emotionally. This is
   deliberate and slightly cruel.
3. On the walk out she gives her line (§2). She does not ask you for anything. She never does.
4. At Fairweather Hall, Lord Rudolpho offers Sera's hand. Sera then does something no one expects: she asks
   the hero, in front of her father, whether he actually wants to — the first time in her life she has asked
   for what *she* wants, out loud.
5. **The menu appears.** Two names. That is all. `WILLOW PYE` / `SERA FAIRWEATHER`.

**The guard rails, because the player is a child:**
- **Cancel always works.** Backing out puts you on the Hall balcony, not into a failure state. You may leave
  Fairweather Hall entirely and walk the world for as long as you like; both flags remain open.
- **You may sleep on it.** Sleeping at any inn while `ch2.pearl` is set plays a 30-second dream: two silhouetted
  futures — a lamp-lit inn kitchen with a slingshot on the wall; a fountain courtyard with music. Neither is
  brighter. Neither is scored. You wake up with no advice.
- **Nobody in the game will tell you which to pick.** Ask Barty and he says: *"That's the one thing I can't
  cook for you, little master."* Ask Bobble and he says: *"Frankly, Bobble would marry the pearl."* The
  hint system in kid-mode (`P33`) explicitly refuses this one question and says so.
- **Three confirmations, escalating in gravity, all cancellable.** "Ask her?" → the girl herself answers → "Are
  you sure? You won't be able to change this." The third prompt uses the full-screen DQ window and takes a
  slower typewriter speed than any other text in the game.

**Why a child will agonise, precisely:**
- **Both are mechanically excellent and non-identical** (numbers: SYSTEMS-BIBLE §2.4). Willow is the
  Firecracker: fast, a whip and boomerang that hit every enemy, the Scorcha fire line and **Whistle Down**
  (fewer battles — the single most quality-of-life skill in the game). Sera is the Slow Bloom: the best healing
  in the game, **Rouse** (always revives) and **Snoozle**. The twins' *spell lists differ by mother* — Willow's
  children learn fire early and Rowan learns Whistle Down; Sera's learn healing early and Linnet learns Rouse —
  and the game tells you this openly. There is no
  correct answer; there are two different, equally strong families.
- **Both futures are visibly good.** No punishment ending. No secret best route.
- **Both girls behave beautifully.** The unchosen one is generous in public and quietly sad in exactly one
  optional line of dialogue, found only if you go back and talk to her alone after the wedding — a line the
  six-year-old will never find and the eleven-year-old will find and never forget.
- **The ribbon.** Whoever you marry, the hero is still wearing half of Willow's ribbon in every single frame of
  Act III. If you married Sera, she knows what it is, has never once asked him to take it off, and mentions
  this only in the epilogue, kindly.

## 7. The ending, and what the epilogue shows

**The last dungeon (B25).** The Quiet Deep is not a hell; it is a *library of people*. Mortmain has, over
forty years, "kept" eleven hundred souls in stone so that they could not be lost — a shepherd, a bride, six
soldiers, a girl with a dog, all of them mid-sentence. Between the two boss phases he stops fighting, sits
down on a step, and tells you about Tobin: seven years old, the coughing sickness, three nights, and a prayer
that nothing answered. *"You've just got your father back, in a manner of speaking. So tell me. Tell me I was
wrong to try."* The hero cannot answer. **Rowan does**, in the flattest nine-year-old voice imaginable:
*"You could have just been sad. Everyone else is."*

**The final blow.** Mortmain is not killed. Malgrim's dream is cut away from him by the Larksteel Sword and
the old man is left kneeling, ordinary, awake, and about eighty years old all at once. He asks, very quietly,
whether they will be angry with him — the eleven hundred, when they wake. Elowen says no, and helps him up.
He is last seen in the epilogue, alive, sweeping the abbey steps at Whistfell. **He is not forgiven and he is
not punished. He is put to work.** A child will find this deeply satisfying and will not know why.

**The waking (B26).** Elowen gives the last of her Skyborne light. It runs through the Stone Garden like sun
through a window: eleven hundred statues going soft in a wave, people finishing sentences they started in
1200-and-something, a dog barking. It costs her the glow — she is now simply a woman, fifty-one years old in
the world's reckoning and twenty-five in her own, and she is fine with that, and she says so. **Your wife wakes
last**, still holding your hand from nine years ago, and looks past you at two nine-year-olds she has never
met, and says: *"…Whose are they?"* And you point at yourself, and then at her, and she puts her hand over her
mouth. **The hero never speaks. The hero points.**

**The epilogue** (playable, ~8 minutes, then a scroll). The player walks freely around Ambergarde Keep and town
on coronation morning and may talk to everyone. Every conversation is a payoff:
- **Barty** in the kitchen, handing the house keys to Linnet: "Right, little miss. First lesson. You don't salt
  a stew before you've tasted it."
- **Bertie**, now a decent and unpompous king, standing awkwardly by the wall, being announced with no titles
  at all and looking rather pleased about it.
- **Bobble**, announced correctly by the herald, sitting down heavily and being fanned by a Quietling who
  has taken a job in the kitchens.
- **Pip**, now a lion the size of a pony, asleep across three chairs, still trying to get on the hero's lap.
- **The unchosen friend** is at the coronation, in her best clothes, and her line to your children is warm and
  funny and contains one word too many, and that word is the whole ache: *"Your dad and I broke into a haunted
  house once, you know. He held my hand the whole way up. I've told that story an awful lot."*
- **Elowen**, on the battlements, teaching Linnet the rest of the lullaby — the player can stand there and
  listen to the whole thing.
- **Mortmain**, if you fly to Whistfell after the credits, sweeping.

**The final shot.** The family on the keep steps for the coronation portrait — the hero, his wife, Rowan with
the Larksteel Sword he is still slightly too small for, Linnet, Elowen, Barty, Bobble, Pip, and whatever
three monsters the player recruited (they are in the portrait; the game remembers their nicknames). The hero
unties the ribbon from his own wrist, kneels, and knots it round **Linnet's** wrist. She looks at it. He nods
twice, fast. **Camera pulls up and back into a wide, bright, cloudless sky** — the same sky Halvard told him to
look at — and holds there while the family theme plays through, complete, with the lullaby countermelody
underneath it. Title card: ***The Lark and the Quiet Hand.*** Then: *"You were away for a while. Welcome home."*
